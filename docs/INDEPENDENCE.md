# Independence and provenance

Meridia is independent research by Robert Sneiderman. Every world is synthetic and
generated by the code in this repository from a seed. No real microdata, no
administrative records, no survey responses, and no material, code, documentation, or
internal method from any employer or government body were used, consulted, or
reproduced. The methods are taken from the public scientific literature and cited
below at the point of use; the parameter ranges of a world's character are declared in
`meridia/character.py`; the defect mechanisms of the observed sources are declared in
`meridia/sources.py` and `meridia/survey.py`.

Two mechanical guards enforce the boundary. The voice gate run by the git hooks blocks
agency vocabulary and the employer's name in every committed file. The packet builder
refuses to write any truth-side column to the participant side.

## Methods and their public sources

- Record deduplication and linkage keys: Fellegi and Sunter (1969), A theory for record
  linkage, JASA 64(328).
- Nonresponse adjustment within sampling units and raking to known margins: Deville and
  Sarndal (1992), Calibration estimators in survey sampling, JASA 87(418); Deming and
  Stephan (1940), On a least squares adjustment of a sampled frequency table, Annals of
  Mathematical Statistics 11(4).
- Synthetic and composite small-area estimation with a size-class coverage model:
  Rao and Molina (2015), Small Area Estimation, 2nd ed., Wiley; Fay and Herriot (1979),
  Estimates of income for small places, JASA 74(366).
- Coverage estimation from two sources: Chandrasekar and Deming (1949), On a method of
  estimating birth and death rates and the extent of registration, JASA 44(245).
- Variance by resampling sampling units within strata: Rao and Wu (1988), Resampling
  inference with complex survey data, JASA 83(401).
- Hot-deck imputation and the treatment of item nonresponse: Rubin (1987), Multiple
  Imputation for Nonresponse in Surveys, Wiley; Andridge and Little (2010), A review of
  hot deck imputation for survey non-response, International Statistical Review 78(1).
- Hierarchical Bayesian estimation for small areas: Gelman et al. (2013), Bayesian Data
  Analysis, 3rd ed., CRC; Rao and Molina (2015), chapter 10.
- Cohort-component population projection and life-table construction: Preston,
  Heuveline, and Guillot (2001), Demography: Measuring and Modeling Population
  Processes, Blackwell; Gompertz (1825) and Makeham (1860) for the mortality law.
- Proper scoring of intervals: Gneiting and Raftery (2007), Strictly proper scoring
  rules, prediction, and estimation, JASA 102(477).
- Suppression and disclosure by linear recovery: Hundepool et al. (2012), Statistical
  Disclosure Control, Wiley; Cox (1980), Suppression methodology and statistical
  disclosure control, JASA 75(370).
- Priority-flood depression filling in the terrain layer: Barnes, Lehman, and Mulla
  (2014), Computers and Geosciences 62.

## What a participant may and may not learn

Participants see the parameter ranges and the mechanism families, never a sealed
world's draw. Development worlds ship with their truth; hidden worlds exist only as
sealed digests until grading. No statistic of a hidden world's full population appears
in any participant file.
